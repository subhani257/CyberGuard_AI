# 🧠 CyberGuard AI — AI Technology Breakdown

For your presentation, if the evaluators ask exactly *what* AI technologies you are using and *where* they fit in the system, use this cheat sheet. It breaks down the 4 core AI components.

---

## 1. spaCy (Traditional NLP)
*   **What it is:** An open-source Natural Language Processing library.
*   **What it does:** Think of it as a "Highlighter Pen." It reads text and identifies specific types of words (Named Entity Recognition - NER).
*   **Our Use Cases:**
    1.  **Data Privacy (Sanitization):** When a company uploads a document, spaCy scans it and masks real emails, phone numbers, and names *before* they are saved to the database. (e.g., replaces `john@company.com` with `[MASKED_EMAIL]`).
    2.  **Threat Extraction:** Before the LLM scores a user, spaCy extracts concrete threat indicators from the scenario (like suspicious URLs or dollar amounts) to give the LLM structured data to evaluate against.

## 2. Transformers (SentenceTransformers)
*   **What it is:** A local, open-source AI model architecture (often downloaded from Hugging Face).
*   **What it does:** Think of it as a "Translator." It reads human text and translates it into mathematical numbers called **Embeddings** (Vectors). It understands the deep semantic *meaning* of a sentence.
*   **Our Use Case:**
    *   **Information Processing for RAG:** We use a local SentenceTransformer model (like `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`) to read all of our NIST and NCSC cybersecurity documents and convert them into vectors so they can be searched later.

## 3. Embeddings & pgvector (The Storage & Search)
*   **What it is:** Embeddings are the numbers generated by the Transformer. **pgvector** is a plugin for PostgreSQL (hosted on Supabase) that stores these numbers.
*   **Where they are saved:** Inside the Supabase PostgreSQL database, in tables specifically configured with vector columns (e.g., our `cyber_training` and `org_knowledge` tables).
*   **Our Use Case:**
    *   **Semantic Search (RAG):** When a user makes a mistake, we convert their mistake into an embedding (using the Transformer). We then ask `pgvector` to search the database and instantly retrieve the 3 most relevant NIST cybersecurity rules that match the meaning of that mistake.

## 4. OpenAI Layer (Generative AI / LLM)
*   **What it is:** A massive, cloud-based Large Language Model (e.g., GPT-4o-mini).
*   **What it does:** Think of it as the "Brain and Writer." It takes all the structured data we gathered and generates human-readable text.
*   **Our Use Cases:**
    1.  **The Scenario Agent:** We give OpenAI the user's role and company workflows (retrieved from the database), and it writes a highly realistic, customized phishing email.
    2.  **The Coach & Evaluation Agents:** We give OpenAI the user's mistake AND the specific NIST rules retrieved by pgvector. OpenAI uses that context to evaluate the user objectively and write personalized coaching feedback explaining *why* they were wrong.

---

### 🔄 The Full Flow (Putting it together)

1.  **spaCy** cleans the company data to protect privacy.
2.  **Transformers** convert that clean data into **Embeddings**.
3.  We save those Embeddings into **Supabase pgvector**.
4.  During training, we retrieve the right data from pgvector and send it to **OpenAI**.
5.  **OpenAI** writes the final scenario and feedback for the user.
